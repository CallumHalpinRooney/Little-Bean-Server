What procurement teams actually ask about data protection

Enterprise buyers have moved from asking whether you comply to asking you to show it. Here is the shape of that request, and what a good answer looks like.

[PLACEHOLDER: author name]

[PLACEHOLDER: role]

Supplier due diligence used to end with a signed data processing agreement. It increasingly starts there. Procurement and vendor risk teams now ask for evidence: who assessed you, against what, when, and what you did about what they found.

The questions behind the questionnaire

Most security questionnaires converge on the same underlying concerns, however they are worded. Read past the format and you tend to find four:

  • Do you know what personal data you hold on our behalf, and where it goes?
  • Can you evidence the controls you claim, rather than assert them?
  • What happens in the first 72 hours of an incident?
  • Are the people handling our data trained, recently, on the things that go wrong?

Assertion versus evidence

The gap that stalls deals is rarely the absence of a policy. It is the absence of a link between the policy and what happens in practice — a retention schedule nobody applies, a training module nobody completed, a processor register that predates two migrations.
